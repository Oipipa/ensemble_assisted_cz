The effective-blockade plot is a qualitative comparison with [Figure 3(c) of Zhang et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16896). The pulse boundaries are 0.05, 0.15, and 0.20 microseconds. The $|A00\rangle$ input completes an ancilla cycle. The other inputs excite data qubits while the ancilla transition is blocked. All inputs return near their starting populations.

This model replaces the resonant Förster pair-state coupling by a diagonal energy shift. It also omits Rydberg decay. Its small residual excitation and phase errors should therefore not be read as a quantitative reproduction of the paper's curves or Table II.

The later noisy-channel calculation follows the Haar-average fidelity definition in Supplementary Section II, Eqs. (4)-(5). It traces out the ancilla but retains the data loss states. The computational output is not renormalized. With the stated lifetimes and effective diagonal blockade, the calculated average fidelity is 0.997351; a reproducible 2000-state Haar sample gives 0.997353. This is below the paper's 0.9980 target, and it is not yet the same physical model.

At the paper's parameters, the simplified model gives isolated data-decay error 0.1498%, ancilla-decay error 0.0299%, and finite-blockade error 0.0852%. [Table II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16896) reports 0.15%, 0.03%, and 0.02%. The first two agree with the integrated-population estimates in Supplementary Eq. (28). The finite-blockade error is larger because a diagonal shift leaves an AC Stark phase, while the resonant Förster model has a protected dark state. The combined noisy-channel fidelity is 99.7351%, compared with the paper's 99.80%. Isolated error contributions are comparisons, not an exact decomposition of the combined channel.

The resonant model starts from Supplementary Eq. (29), using basis $|1R\rangle,|rR\rangle,|r'R'\rangle$. The full gate uses ancilla basis $|A\rangle,|R\rangle,|R'\rangle$ and each data-atom basis $|0\rangle,|1\rangle,|r\rangle,|r'\rangle$, with tensor order ancilla, data 1, data 2. Only the resonant pair coupling $|rR\rangle\leftrightarrow|r'R'\rangle$ is added; extra pair levels, $V_{Ar}$, decay, and spatial variation remain absent. At $V/\Omega=20$, peak subsystem populations are 0.000625 in $|rR\rangle$ and 0.002497 in $|r'R'\rangle$. The latter is transient interference between the dark and bright components of the initial bare state. The truncated full gate has maximum computational leakage 0.000773 and matrix deviation 0.000386 from Eq. (4).

Supplementary Eq. (30) predicts a zero-energy dark state proportional to $2V_{Rr}|1R\rangle-\Omega_r|r'R'\rangle$. Its two paths to $|rR\rangle$ cancel. Direct diagonalization gives eigenvalues $0$ and $\pm\sqrt{\Omega_r^2+4V_{Rr}^2}/2$ to within $3.87\times10^{-12}$ radians per microsecond. The dark-state overlap is one to numerical precision. As $V_{Rr}/\Omega_r$ grows, the initial bare $|1R\rangle$ has greater weight in this protected state.

Under the same square pulses and $V_{Rr}/\Omega_r=20$, perfect projection gives zero leakage and phase error. The finite diagonal model gives leakage $1.5\times10^{-5}$ and a 0.078393-rad phase error. The truncated Förster model gives leakage $7.73\times10^{-4}$ but no measurable phase error. The diagonal shift is therefore inadequate for phase-sensitive fidelity estimates even when its population leakage appears small. The truncated Förster model still lacks decay and extra pair states.
